# Personal Reflection
The thing I find most surprising is that AI is useful not just to write code, but also to test it, debug it, etc. Before this research, I thought of it as just a tool for suggesting code to the programmer. It turns out it is also useful to generate test cases, explain error messages, suggest fixes and generally aid programmers throughout the entire process of developing a software including the CI/CD process.

Another thing that surprises me is that the AI speeds up the process, but is not able to comprehend the true objective of the software. The AI could potentially generate a test case which appears to be valid and, possibly, pass, yet the software may not be correctly doing what it's supposed to be doing according to the actual requirement. This, as well, helped me realize that software engineering is more than just about producing code or tests; it also has to do with understanding the problem, its requirement, and the system's behavior.
## 1. What surprised me most
The risk that I'm most worried about is developers may trust too quickly an AI's suggestion. If an AI creates a test or proposes a solution it may look right at first glance but the solution can be still incomplete or outright incorrect. This is problematic as it would cause developers to stop rigorously checking their work and blindly accepting the output of the AI without fully understanding why it is being suggested.

Another problem I'm worried about is that juniors may become too reliant on AI. If novice developers use an AI to obtain simple answers then they won't be improving their own ability to test, debug and problem-solve. In engineering this is a huge issue as the engineer will be held responsible for the final code. While AI can be useful, the output should not substitute the understanding of the developer. I feel the biggest problem is not the existence of AI, but the misuse of AI without a critical approach.

# Personal Reflection
The thing I find most surprising is that AI is useful not just to write code, but also to test it, debug it, etc. Before this research, I thought of it as just a tool for suggesting code to the programmer. It turns out it is also useful to generate test cases, explain error messages, suggest fixes and generally aid programmers throughout the entire process of developing a software including the CI/CD process.

Another thing that surprises me is that the AI speeds up the process, but is not able to comprehend the true objective of the software. The AI could potentially generate a test case which appears to be valid and, possibly, pass, yet the software may not be correctly doing what it's supposed to be doing according to the actual requirement. This, as well, helped me realize that software engineering is more than just about producing code or tests; it also has to do with understanding the problem, its requirement, and the system's behavior.

## 1. What surprised me most

The risk that I'm most worried about is developers may trust too quickly an AI's suggestion. If an AI creates a test or proposes a solution it may look right at first glance but the solution can be still incomplete or outright incorrect. This is problematic as it would cause developers to stop rigorously checking their work and blindly accepting the output of the AI without fully understanding why it is being suggested.

Another problem I'm worried about is that juniors may become too reliant on AI. If novice developers use an AI to obtain simple answers then they won't be improving their own ability to test, debug and problem-solve. In engineering this is a huge issue as the engineer will be held responsible for the final code. While AI can be useful, the output should not substitute the understanding of the developer. I feel the biggest problem is not the existence of AI, but the misuse of AI without a critical approach.

## 2. What concerns me about AI in software engineering

Perhaps the thing that I worry about most when it comes to AI in software engineering is the fact that engineers could blindly follow what the AI suggests. For example if AI suggests a test case, or an error explanation or suggests a solution for that error, then it could still be wrong and incomplete but could appear to be correct to begin with. In such cases a developer could simply stop considering why something is correct/not correct and simply apply whatever is provided by the AI.
Another thing I am worried about is thatjunior engineers can over rely on AI. This is a great disadvantage for young engineers who just start to be in the engineering role-if they always ask for simple and instant answer then they will never learn the proper technique for testing, debugging and solving problems. This is indeed a great trouble since the engineer still has to make sure what the software is doing is correct at the end of the process, and the programmer has to take responsibility even if AI assisted the process.
Finally, the security and quality of the code generated by an AI are another concern of mine. For instance, it may solve one error and leave another, or it may work just in one trivial scenario. Due to this reason the suggestions made by the AI can not and should not be trusted. I do not think that AI in the software engineering process is bad but rather how the engineers may approach AI in this field that are problematic.
## 3. Opportunities I see

I can see an opportunity in the way that software engineering tests and debugging can be accelerated using AI. The opportunity arises here as an engineer will not have to start test case generation or debugging an error from scratch if AI is able to create the initial test cases, or explain why the code has thrown an error. This means that time can be used more efficiently allowing the engineer to better verify if the solution provided is indeed correct.

Another opportunity is with regard to learning for more novice software engineers if used correctly. For instance when AI explains a reason for a certain bug or points to a bug within the code, a novice engineer will be able to use this to quickly gain an understanding of why an error occurred, and correct the mistake; This is an opportunity if the engineer actually learns the reason for the bug rather than simply Copy-Pasting the suggested fix.

Another opportunity I see with AI is with regard to teamwork. With large projects and code from other engineers, AI could have the potential to provide an insight or explanation into areas of code which are confusing, thus increasing the ease of maintenance and the identification of problems early on, rather than after software release.

All in all, I believe that AI can be an opportunity to increase efficiency, the ability to learn, and improve quality, but it must be implemented properly. The optimum opportunity that I see is the assistance of an engineer in performing tasks more efficiently, rather than replacing the engineer.
## 4. Skills that will remain important

4. Skills That Will Remain Relevant

Even with the development of AI, I believe strong programming fundamentals will continue to be critical for software engineers. AI will likely be able to suggest code, tests or bug fixes, but it will be the engineer that must ultimately determine whether these are correct. If an engineer lacks a solid grasp of programming logic, data structures, algorithms and syntax they may readily accept incorrect suggestions as being correct and introduce bugs or inefficiencies.

Testing and debugging will also remain critical skills. Although AI may be capable of generating tests or suggesting potential bug locations, it is not always possible for AI to truly understand the true requirement of the software. An engineer must still be able to test edge cases, determine if output is correct, and reliably reproduce and confirm fixes for bugs.

Understanding requirements will be another critical skill. Software engineering is much more than just writing code, it involves understanding the requirement from the user or the system. While an AI might be able to understand and work with the code itself, it doesn't understand the business need for the software or the overall intention of the software. Therefore communication, analytical and problem solving skills will remain paramount.

I believe the best software engineers of the future will be those who can combine strong technical fundamentals with an intelligent and critical approach to utilizing AI, knowing when to question its suggestions and how to test its results thoroughly.
